# Foreword

When I first decided to learn Go, I was confused about where to start. There are so many languages out there. Python, JavaScript, Rust, Java, C++. Why Go? What makes it special? And more importantly, how do I actually learn it in a way that sticks?

I tried watching random YouTube videos. I read blog posts that assumed I already knew everything. I tried to build APIs without understanding how HTTP works. It was frustrating. I kept jumping from topic to topic without building a solid foundation.

But then something changed. I realized that to truly understand Go, I needed to understand the fundamentals. Not just the syntax, but why things work the way they do. Why does Go have goroutines instead of threads? Why is there no inheritance? Why does the garbage collector work the way it does? These questions led me down a rabbit hole of computer science concepts that actually made me a better programmer.

**Why I am writing this:**

I am writing this for myself first. Every time I learn something new, I write it here so I can come back to it later. But I also hope that if you are starting your own Go journey, these notes might save you some of the confusion I went through.

**What I believe about learning:**

- You do not need to be a CS graduate to learn Go
- Writing code is more important than watching code
- Understanding the "why" is more important than memorizing the "how"
- Making mistakes is the fastest way to learn
- Consistency beats intensity. Learning 1 hour daily is better than 10 hours once a week

**A note about these notes:**

I am not claiming this is complete or perfect. There are gaps. There are probably mistakes too. But this is a living document that I keep updating as I learn more. If you spot something wrong, that is great. It means you understood it well enough to catch the error.

These notes cover Go from the very basics to building production-ready backend systems with databases, authentication, and clean architecture. I did not learn all of this in a few weeks. It took months, and I am still learning. Do not try to rush through everything. Take your time with each section. Make sure you can write the code yourself before moving on.

I hope this helps you on your journey. Remember, every expert was once a beginner who refused to give up.
